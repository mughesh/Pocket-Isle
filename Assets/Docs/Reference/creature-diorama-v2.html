Three body plans, one toon look

Each creature's body is one smooth sculpted mesh, made by melting soft shapes together in code. Toon shading, outlines and painted eyes are shaders. Ears, tails and wings are separate pieces that move, so nothing needs a skeleton.

Why it scales

A creature is a short recipe of shapes: body, head, limbs, tail. Change the recipe and you get a new silhouette. Body plans (four-legged, blob, bird, and later serpent, biped, floater) each become a template you vary.

Why it won't break

No bones or skin weights. The body is static and only breathes or jiggles in the shader. Moving parts are rigid pieces on pivots. If a recipe looks wrong, you tweak a number and re-sculpt.

Battle effects

Fireball, splash, gust, shockwave and shield are small shaders plus one shared particle pool. Every attack is one of four templates (shoot, lunge, slam, shield) recoloured per element, so new creatures reuse them.

For Quest

Sculpting happens once at build time in Unity, then it's a normal mesh. Bodies here are about 9–11k triangles; Unity's mesh simplifier can halve that. Outlines double draw calls in this preview, but they merge in Unity.
